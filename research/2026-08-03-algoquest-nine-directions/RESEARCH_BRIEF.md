# AlgoQuest: nine directions, one governed Hero Book research line

Date: 2026-08-03  
Status: research and authoring preparation; no learner deployment

## Objective

Prepare a 62-source research bank for the nine AlgoQuest product directions. AlgoQuest remains the autonomous foundation and its alpha does not depend on the Hero Book system. Hero Books are an active development line through the end of beta. The canonical research plan is 9 main axes plus 30 bounded sub-researches: 12 fixed sources support directions 1-6, 15 user-supplied sources begin directions 7-8, and 35 sources remain to be acquired and verified.

## Environment / Stack Context

AlgoQuest is a pre-alpha Vite/React application with separate student and teacher surfaces, typed local contracts, and no live suite API broker, production telemetry sink, or end-to-end inter-tool proof. The initial Hero Book experience runs in Google Colab with an accessible ASCII visual grammar. Algorithm Builder App is an optional AlgoQuest construction plugin for selected quests; it is not a mandatory dependency for the foundation. This research does not authorize runtime, learner-data, or school deployment changes.

## Research Questions

1. How can a 240-prompt Hero Book select forty unique prompts per audience-specific adventure without becoming a fixed page-turning book?
2. How can the story and education views share a quest assignment without treating story points as proof of learning?
3. How can validated evidence support a real school-year path and transferable foundations without creating grades, diagnoses, or unreviewed claims?

## Findings

**Confirmed by primary sources.** Existing interactive-fiction tools document addressable narrative nodes, variables, conditions, testing, localization, and accessibility constraints. Official Gemini documentation supports structured output and safety configuration, but neither capability makes a model an authority for learner progress.

**Inferred from multiple secondary sources.** Narrative/game design, learning feedback, UDL, project-based learning, and gamification literature together support a design that separates narrative motivation, observable artifacts, accessibility options, and curricular evidence.

**Tentative due to missing learner trials.** The precise forty-prompt allocation policy, the six audience adaptations, the story universes, and the `UnderstandingPath` unlock thresholds require authored fixtures, synthetic runs, educator review, accessibility review, and later human validation.

## Alternatives Considered

- A literal page-turning branch book: rejected as the core runtime because it cannot select prompts by evidence, accommodation, current project, or school-year objective.
- Fully generative quest creation: rejected as canonical progression because it cannot guarantee provenance, eligibility, repeatability, or assessment boundaries.
- A single game/education interface: rejected because it hides the difference between narrative reward and validated evidence.

## Risks / Unknowns

- A source bank is not a curriculum pack, a cultural review, a safety review, or proof of learning efficacy.
- Historical Hero Book settings need culture- and period-specific research before authoring.
- AI model output, shared notebook state, and externally hosted tools remain non-canonical until separately governed.
